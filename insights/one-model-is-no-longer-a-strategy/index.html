One model is no longer a strategy

By Frank Ribeiro, Jengara · 1 November 2026 · Product and engineering notes

A model portfolio assigns every workload to the cheapest model that meets its quality, latency and risk bar, and keeps the frontier model for the steps that need it. That is no longer a practice of a few large platforms. It is how most production AI already runs, how the clouds now sell models, and where the next cost and governance failures will come from.

Research approach

Jengara reviewed the Datadog State of AI Engineering 2026 report, built on telemetry from more than a thousand production customers, the LangChain Switchyard routing benchmark from August 2026, and the Google Cloud announcement of OpenAI models in its model garden on 21 October 2026. Every figure traces to those sources, listed below.

The portfolio is already the default

Datadog found more than 70 percent of organisations now use three or more models, and the share using more than six models nearly doubled in a year. OpenAI held a 63 percent share, down from 75 percent a year earlier, even as the number of customers using OpenAI more than doubled, because rival providers grew faster. Rather than picking a single default, teams are building model portfolios so each workload gets the model that fits its latency, cost and risk profile. The fleet arrived by accumulation, though. Teams are quick to test new releases and slow to retire models already in production, so portfolios only grow, and the evaluation burden grows with them.

Most calls never needed the frontier model

LangChain ran its Deep Agents evaluation suite through NVIDIA NeMo Switchyard, an open source routing library. The suite holds 145 multistep tasks averaging 6.3 model calls each. The router sent only 7 percent of turns to the frontier model, Claude Opus 4.8. A 30 billion parameter open model, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, handled the other 93 percent of calls for 10.4 percent of the spend. The routed run cost 3.00 dollars against 11.45 dollars for the frontier model alone, a saving of 74 percent.

The lesson is not that small models match frontier models. It is that a workload is a mix. Reading a file and working out why a test fails are both model calls, and in a single model setup both are billed at the same rate. Only one of them earns frontier pricing.

The clouds now sell the portfolio for you

On 21 October 2026, Google Cloud added the newest OpenAI models to its model garden, in private preview for selected enterprise customers. Google says the garden now spans more than 200 first party, third party and open models, and that it is the only hyperscale cloud provider offering access to all three leading closed source model families. The three OpenAI tiers map the portfolio idea onto a single family. Astra serves demanding coding, computer use and research, Sol offers close to Astra intelligence at lower cost, and Luna is the fast, cost efficient model for everyday work at scale. Transacted through Google Cloud Marketplace, usage lands on a single Google Cloud invoice, draws down existing cloud commitments and counts toward Flexible Savings Plans across model providers. A rival model is now a line item inside a contract the customer already holds.

Routing has its own bill

Routing is not free, and LangChain prints its price beside its savings. Routed accuracy came in at 80.0 percent against 86.0 percent for the frontier model alone, a real six point gap on this suite. The judge model that decides escalation took 21.2 percent of routed spend, the second largest line item. Frontier traffic moved between runs, from 4.1 percent to 9.1 percent of turns, and the bill moved with it, from 2.16 dollars to 3.61 dollars per run. LangChain advises teams to budget against the top of that range. A portfolio needs three controls before it needs a fourth model: an evaluation set for each workload stage, a spend ceiling for each task type, and an escalation rule that a person has approved in advance.

Retire models like you retire software

The unmanaged portfolio fails in predictable places. Datadog notes that the same prompts, tools and workflows can produce different results across models, so each added model brings its own quality, latency and cost profile, and model churn becomes a governance problem. Capacity is the second failure. In February 2026, 5 percent of model call spans in the Datadog data reported an error, and 60 percent of those errors were caused by exceeded rate limits. A portfolio spreads that risk only when traffic can actually move between providers. Give every model an owner, and record its workload, its evaluation, its cost per successful task and its retirement date. Review the fleet on a calendar rather than at renewal, and remove any route that no current evaluation can justify.

Jengara decision rule

Approve no new model without naming the workload it serves, the evaluation it must keep passing, the owner who watches its cost per successful task, and the date its place in the portfolio gets reviewed. Keep the frontier model for the steps that earn it. Route the rest on evidence, and retire on schedule. One model is a dependency. An unmanaged fleet is a liability. A governed portfolio is a strategy.

Executive takeaway

The executive question has moved from which model to how the fleet is run. Portfolios cut cost and spread risk only when routing, evaluation and retirement are managed as one system.
